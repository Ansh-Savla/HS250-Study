Module Quick Reference

One-line recall per module with key concepts

M01 Sociology of Environment

Why Care? 5 lenses — technology, perception, response, dependence, organization. Utilitarian vs Intrinsic value. MDGs (8 goals, 2015 target) vs HDI (health, education, income) vs Sustainable Development.

M02 Environment & Development

Stockholm 1972 dual agenda (environment + development). Brundtland SD: "needs of present without compromising future." Venn (weak/political) vs Nested (strong/material) diagrams.

M03 Views of Sustainability

Weak (substitutable capitals) vs Strong (non-substitutable natural capital) vs Deep Green (intrinsic value). Haughton's 5 equity principles. Status Quo / Reform / Transformation approaches.

M04 History of Environmentalism

Silent Spring (1962) → Bhopal (1984). North-South divide. Environmentalism of the Poor (Agarwal, Guha). Chipko (1973), KPL. Waves: conservation → pollution → justice.

M05 Tribal Ecologies

Iron Triangle: omnivores (elite consumers), ecosystem people (subsistence), ecological refugees (displaced). Tribal: land as ontological core. Sacred groves as cultural conservation.

M06 Environmental Economics

I = P × A × T. Public goods (non-excludable, non-rival). Tragedy of commons. Prisoner's dilemma. Externalities: negative (S>P), positive (P>S). Private vs Social costs.

M07 Air, Water & Waste Pollution

Air: primary/secondary pollutants, criteria pollutants (PM, SO₂, NOx, CO, O₃, Pb), TRIC, smog types, acid deposition. Water: point vs non-point. Groundwater depletion. Ozone: CFCs, Montreal Protocol.

M08 Environmental Policy Instruments

CAC (standards, bans, tech mandates) vs Market-based (taxes, permits, subsidies). Coase theorem. Polluter Pays Principle. SO₂ allowances (US Acid Rain). Comparison table: goal fulfillment, efficiency, admin ease.

M09 Global Warming & Kyoto

GHGs (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, F-gases). UNFCCC → Kyoto Protocol. Mechanisms: allowance-based (domestic allowances, JI) + project-based (CDM). Emissions trading. CBDR principle.

M10 Philosophy: Greeks-Modern

Pre-Socratics → Plato (Forms) → Aristotle (teleology). Hargrove: Greeks saw eco-phenomena as perishable. Descartes: dualism, primary/secondary qualities, God sustains change. Kant: sublime. Heidegger: enframing.

M11 Modern Env Philosophy

Science critique (Cartesian). Logical positivism, fact/value split. Hume (is/ought). Darwin (continuity). Environmental philosophy 1970s (Routley, Passmore). Deep ecology roots (Naess).

M12 Indian/Buddhist/Ecofeminism

Realist (Samkhya, Nyaya) vs Idealist (Vedanta, Buddhist). Sentience criteria. Buddhist: dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda), impermanence, experiential knowing. Panchsheel. Ecofeminism: dualisms (Nature/Culture, Body/Mind), logic of domination.

Formula Wall

Key equations, definitions, and worked formulas

I = P × A × T

Environmental Impact = Population × Affluence × Technology

All three multiply — no single factor sufficient alone. T = impact per unit consumption.

Externalities

Negative: Social Cost > Private Cost

Positive: Private Cost > Social Cost

Social Cost = Private Cost + External Cost. Pigouvian tax = MEC at Q*.

Tragedy of Commons

Non-excludable + Rivalrous → Overexploitation

Self-interest > Social interest. Solutions: privatize, regulate, community govern (Ostrom).

Brundtland SD

"Meets needs of present without compromising future generations' ability to meet their own needs"

Coase Theorem

If property rights defined + transaction costs ≈ 0 → bargaining achieves efficient outcome regardless of initial allocation

Real world: high transaction costs → allocation matters.

Polluter Pays Principle

Polluter should bear cost of pollution prevention/control

OECD 1972. Internalizes external cost via taxes/permits/liability.

HDI Formula

HDI = (Health Index × Education Index × Income Index)^(1/3)

Health: life expectancy. Education: mean/expected schooling. Income: GNI per capita (log).

MDGs vs SDGs

MDGs: 8 goals, 2015 target, developing focus

SDGs: 17 goals, 2030 target, universal, integrated (5Ps).

Exam Traps — Quick Reference

Trap: "Sustainable development" ≠ "sustainable growth." Growth = quantitative increase; Development = qualitative improvement.
Trap: Intrinsic value ≠ "no human use." Intrinsic = value independent of use, not prohibition of use.
Trap: Gandhi's "poverty = greatest polluters" means poverty forces degradation, not that poor are to blame.
Trap: Venn = weak SD (substitutable); Nested = strong SD (non-substitutable). Don't confuse the arrows.
Trap: HDI and GDP both anthropocentric — neither captures ecological limits. SD adds that dimension.
Trap: "Sustainable development" integrates environment + socio-economic; it's not "environment plus development" as separate silos.
Trap: Negative externality = Social Cost > Private Cost. Positive = Private Cost > Social Cost. Don't flip.
Trap: Coase theorem: property rights solve externality IF transaction costs low. Real world = high transaction costs.
Trap: Permits fulfill goals better than taxes IF market not too thin. Taxes = price certainty, Permits = quantity certainty.
Trap: Deep ecology ≠ environmentalism. Deep ecology = non-human nature has intrinsic rights independent of human utility.
Trap: "Ecosystem people" ≠ "ecological refugees." Ecosystem people = subsistence users; refugees = displaced by projects.
Trap: Ostrom's design principles ≠ government ownership. They work for COMMUNITY/COMMON PROPERTY, not state property.
Trap: Montreal Protocol (ozone) ≠ Kyoto (climate). Montreal = universal, successful; Kyoto = differentiated, mixed.
Trap: JI (Joint Implementation) = Annex I ↔ Annex I. CDM = Annex I → Non-Annex I. Don't swap.
Trap: Descartes: God sustains change → no human nature-preservation needed. This is the anti-ecological consequence of dualism.
Trap: Kant's sublime = we project our reason onto nature → morality. Not "nature is beautiful." Science view disconnects; sublime reconnects via ethics.
Trap: Heidegger: technology is not instrumental; it's a way nature reveals itself (enframing). Problems of tech can't be solved technologically.
Trap: Buddhist dependent origination = universe as mutual web. Harm to nature = harm to self. Not "stewardship" (implies separation).
Trap: Ecofeminism's "oppressive conceptual framework" = value-hierarchical (X>Y), dualisms (Nature/Culture), logic of domination. "Egalitarian partnership" is the SOLUTION, not the framework.
Trap: Panchsheel = 5 principles (mutual respect, non-aggression, etc.) for tribal policy, NOT the 5 precepts of Buddhism.

Module-Specific Key Points

Detailed recall items per module for exam writing

M01: Sociology — Definitions & Concepts
  • Why Care (5 lenses): Technology (cultural response), Perception (societal), Response (power/institutions), Dependence (nature as life support), Organization (material interventions)
  • Utilitarian vs Intrinsic: Utilitarian = instrumental, human-centered; Intrinsic = value in itself, non-human centered
  • MDGs (8): Eradicate poverty, universal primary education, gender equality, child mortality, maternal health, combat HIV/AIDS, environmental sustainability, global partnership
  • HDI: 3 dimensions — health (life expectancy), education (schooling), income (GNI/capita). Geometric mean.
  • Development definitions: Economic growth (GDP), human development (HDI), sustainable development (Brundtland)
M02: Env & Development — Stockholm & Brundtland
  • Stockholm 1972: First UN environment conference. Dual agenda: environment AND development. "Only One Earth."
  • Brundtland (1987): WCED report "Our Common Future." SD = needs of present w/o compromising future. 3 pillars: needs, limits, future generations.
  • Venn diagram: 3 overlapping circles (Econ, Soc, Env). "Political Reality." Weak SD = substitutable.
  • Nested diagram: Concentric: Env > Soc > Econ. "Material Reality." Strong SD = non-substitutable.
  • Left arrow (Venn): "Diverts attention from important questions." Right arrow: "Political Reality → SD."
M03: Sustainability Views — Weak/Strong/Deep
  • Weak SD (Solow/Hartwick): Capitals substitutable. Maintain total capital stock (K_m + K_n constant). Tech optimism.
  • Strong SD (Daly, Costanza): Natural capital non-substitutable. Critical natural capital must be maintained. Scale matters.
  • Deep Green: Intrinsic value of nature. Non-anthropocentric. Biocentrism/ecocentrism.
  • Haughton's 5 Equity: Futurity (inter-gen), Social Justice (intra-gen), Trans-frontier (geo), Procedural (process), Interspecies (bio).
  • Status Quo / Reform / Transformation: Status quo = tech fixes; Reform = efficiency + regulation; Transformation = systemic + limits.
M04: Environmentalism History — Waves & India
  • Silent Spring (1962): Rachel Carson. DDT, bioaccumulation. Catalyst for modern env movement.
  • Bhopal (1984): Union Carbide MIC leak. World's worst industrial disaster. Corporate accountability.
  • North-South divide: North = historical emissions, capacity; South = development needs, vulnerability. CBDR.
  • Environmentalism of the Poor (Agarwal/Guha): Survival ecology vs luxury ecology. Poor = victims + agents of conservation.
  • Chipko (1973): "Hug the trees." Women-led. Subsistence vs commercial forestry.
  • KPL (Kerala People's Science Movement): Science for people, participatory.
M05: Tribal Ecologies — Iron Triangle & FRA
  • Omnivores: ~10-15% elite. High resource access. Displace costs to others.
  • Ecosystem People: Subsistence. Direct resource dependence. Traditional ecological knowledge.
  • Ecological Refugees: Displaced by dams, mines, parks. Loss of land = loss of identity.
  • Tribal perspective: "Land is the ontological core of a tribe's being and fullness." Sacred groves = cultural + biodiversity.
  • FRA 2006: Recognizes individual + community forest rights. Gram sabha as authority.
  • PESA 1996: Extends Panchayats to scheduled areas. Gram sabha powers over resources.
M06: Economics — Public Goods, Externalities, Commons
  • Public goods: Non-excludable + Non-rival (e.g., clean air, ozone layer). Market failure = underprovision.
  • Common Pool Resources: Non-excludable + Rival (e.g., fisheries, groundwater). Tragedy of commons.
  • Club goods: Excludable + Non-rival (e.g., toll road).
  • Private goods: Excludable + Rival.
  • Externalities: Negative (pollution: S>P), Positive (vaccination: P>S). Pigouvian tax = MEC at Q*.
  • Prisoner's Dilemma: Individual rationality → collective suboptimal. Repeated games → cooperation possible.
  • Free rider problem: Non-excludability → benefit without paying.
M07: Pollution — Air, Water, Ozone
  • Criteria pollutants (6): PM₂.₅, PM₁₀, SO₂, NOx, CO, O₃, Pb. NAAQS standards.
  • TRIC conditions: Temperature inversion, Radiation (night cooling), Inversion layer, Calm winds → trap pollutants.
  • Smog types: London (SO₂ + PM, cool/damp), Los Angeles (photochemical: NOx+VOCs+sun, hot).
  • Acid deposition: SO₂, NOx → H₂SO₄, HNO₃. Wet (rain) + Dry (particles). Damages forests, lakes, buildings.
  • Water — Point vs Non-point: Point = pipe; Non-point = runoff. BOD, DO, eutrophication (N, P).
  • Groundwater: Overdraft → subsidence, saltwater intrusion. Recharge slow.
  • Ozone depletion: CFCs → Cl radicals → O₃ destruction. Montreal Protocol (1987) = successful phase-out.
M08: Policy Instruments — CAC vs Market
  • CAC (Command & Control): Standards (emission, ambient, tech), bans, permits. Easy admin, rigid, no innovation incentive.
  • Market-based: Taxes (effluent), Tradable permits (cap-and-trade), Subsidies, Liability, Deposit-refund.
  • Coase Theorem: Bargaining solves if rights defined + low transaction costs. Reality = high costs.
  • Polluter Pays: Internalize cost. OECD 1972. Implemented via taxes/permits/liability.
  • SO₂ Allowance Trading (US 1990): Cap at 8.9M tons. Annual auctions + trading. Success: 50% reduction, low cost.
  • Comparison: Permits = goal fulfillment (quantity certainty). Taxes = price certainty. CAC = firm-level only.
M09: Global Warming — Kyoto Mechanisms
  • GHGs: CO₂ (76%), CH₄ (16%), N₂O (6%), F-gases (2%). GWP: CO₂=1, CH₄=28, N₂O=265.
  • UNFCCC (1992): Stabilize GHGs. CBDR. Annex I (developed) vs Non-Annex I.
  • Kyoto (1997): Annex I binding targets (avg -5.2% 1990 levels, 2008-12). US didn't ratify.
  • Flexibility Mechanisms: (1) Allowance-based: Domestic allowances (cap-and-trade), JI (Annex I→Annex I). (2) Project-based: CDM (Annex I→Non-Annex I, generates CERs).
  • CDM: Sustainable development + emission reduction. Additionality required.
  • Paris (2015): NDCs, bottom-up, 1.5-2°C, global stocktake.
M10: Greeks to Modern — Descartes, Kant, Heidegger
  • Greeks (Hargrove): Eco-phenomena = perishable, unworthy of highest knowledge. Suspicion of sense-knowledge.
  • Plato: Forms > particulars. Nature as imperfect copy. Anti-empirical.
  • Aristotle: Teleology (final cause). Nature has purpose. But privileged metaphysics over physics.
  • Descartes: Dualism (res cogitans / res extensa). Primary qualities (math) vs secondary (sensory). "God sustains change" → no human nature-preservation needed. Animals = machines.
  • Kant: Sublime = fear → project reason onto nature → experience own rationality → morality. Fact/value split.
  • Heidegger: Technology = enframing (Gestell). Nature as standing reserve. Problems of tech ≠ tech solutions.
M11: Modern Env Philosophy — Science Critique, Deep Ecology
  • Cartesian science critique: Reductionism, objectification, domination. Nature as machine.
  • Logical positivism: Verification principle. Ethics = emotive, non-cognitive. Fact/value split entrenched.
  • Hume: Is/ought gap. Cannot derive values from facts.
  • Darwin: Continuity (humans = animals). Undermines human exceptionalism.
  • Env philosophy 1970s: Routley "last man" thought experiment. Passmore "man's responsibility for nature."
  • Deep Ecology (Naess): Platform: intrinsic value, diversity, vital needs, population reduction, non-interference. Self-realization = identification with nature.
  • Shallow vs Deep: Shallow = resource management for humans. Deep = biospheric egalitarianism.
M12: Indian/Buddhist/Ecofeminism
  • Realist schools: Samkhya (prakriti/purusha), Nyaya (logic, atomism), Vaisheshika. World real, independent.
  • Idealist schools: Vedanta (Brahman), Buddhist (dependent origination, no-self). World = appearance/mind-dependent.
  • Sentience criteria: Pain/pleasure capacity. Animals = sentient. Plants = debated.
  • Buddhist: Pratītyasamutpāda (dependent origination) = mutual web. Anicca (impermanence). Anatta (no-self). Experiential knowing > abstract.
  • Panchsheel (Nehru 1958): 5 principles for tribal policy: (1) Non-imposition, (2) Respect rights, (3) Develop own genius, (4) Administer through own institutions, (5) Judge by human character not statistics.
  • Ecofeminism: Oppressive framework = value-hierarchical (X>Y), dualisms (Nature/Culture, Body/Mind, Emotion/Reason), logic of domination. Solution = egalitarian partnership.
  • Key figures: Shiva (Vandana), Mies, Plumwood, Warren.
