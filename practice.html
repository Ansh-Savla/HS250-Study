Sociology Module (from HS 200 End-Sem)

SOC1. Guha and Gadgil show that Omnivores corner the benefits of economic progress at the cost of the vast majority who are ecosystem people and ecological refugees. The omnivores constitute:

✓ Correct Answer: D. None of the above

SOC2. According to Elinor Ostrom, ________ refer to an "element or condition that helps to account for the success of institutions in sustaining the common-pool resource and gaining the compliance of generation after generation of appropriators to the rules in use".

✓ Correct Answer: B. Design Principles

SOC3. In India, multi-purpose dams like Sardar Sarovar have conflicting purposes. Which statement is WRONG?

✓ Correct Answer: D. Flooding prevented by maintaining reservoir levels during post-monsoon when flooding possibility is greater

SOC4. Vandana Shiva argues high-yielding varieties (HYV) of wheat may yield 40% more but require _______ as much water:

✓ Correct Answer: A. Four times

SOC5. Vandana Shiva states Green Revolution reduced genetic diversity by replacing mixtures/rotations of wheat, maize, millets, pulses, oilseeds with _______ of wheat and rice:

✓ Correct Answer: A. Monocultures

SOC6. Guha and Gadgil suggest the organizational unit for management of common lands should be the:

✓ Correct Answer: A. Village

SOC7. Ostrom's "collective choice arrangements" design principle means:

✓ Correct Answer: A. Most individuals affected by operational rules can participate in modifying operational rules

SOC8. In the Plachimada Coca-Cola case, the Kerala High Court used the ________ doctrine:

✓ Correct Answer: A. Public Trust doctrine

SOC9. Arundhati Roy estimates ~_____ million people displaced by big dams in last 50 years in India:

✓ Correct Answer: A. 44

SOC10. Due to seasonal rainfall, three major purposes of dams conflict: irrigation, ________ and ________.

✓ Correct Answer: A. power production and flood-control

SOC11. In "Liquid City", Arjun Appadurai states clean water is the basis for revolution in:

✓ Correct Answer: A. Public Health

Lecture Notes Application Questions

All 12 modules — scenario-based

N1. A city plans a new metro line. Environmental clearance requires assessing impact using I=P×A×T. The city argues: "Technology (electric trains) reduces T, so impact is low." What's the flaw?

✓ Correct Answer: All of the above

N2. A factory pollutes a river used by downstream farmers. Government considers: (a) emission standard, (b) effluent tax, (c) tradable permits. Which gives regulator MOST certainty of achieving a specific pollution target?

✓ Correct Answer: Tradable permits

N3. A tribal community's sacred grove is threatened by a mining lease. The community argues under Forest Rights Act. Which concept from Module 5 best supports their claim?

✓ Correct Answer: Tribal ecologies — land as ontological core of being

N4. Using the Venn vs Nested SD diagrams: A state government proposes "compensatory afforestation" — planting trees elsewhere to offset forest cleared for a dam. Which diagram critique applies?

✓ Correct Answer: Nested diagram rejects this (no substitution for environment)

N5. A policy maker cites Brundtland: "Development meets present needs without compromising future." They argue a coal plant meets "present energy needs." What's missing?

✓ Correct Answer: All of the above

N6. Descartes' dualism (mind vs matter) leads to anti-ecological philosophy. Which worked example from Module 10 shows this?

✓ Correct Answer: God responsible for change → no human nature-preservation needed

N7. Buddhist philosophy's "dependent origination" (pratītyasamutpāda) implies which environmental ethic?

✓ Correct Answer: Universe as mutual web of relationships — harm to nature = harm to self

N8. Ecofeminism's "oppressive conceptual framework" includes all EXCEPT:

✓ Correct Answer: Egalitarian partnership model

N9. Kyoto Protocol's Clean Development Mechanism (CDM) is an example of:

✓ Correct Answer: Project-based mechanism

N10. In Hargrove's view, Greek philosophy prevented nature-friendly attitudes because:

✓ Correct Answer: Greeks saw eco-phenomena as perishable, unworthy of highest knowledge; privileged reason over observation

N11. Kant's "sublime" experience of nature leads to environmental ethics because:

✓ Correct Answer: We overcome fear by projecting sublimity onto nature → recognize our rational/ethical capacity → morality

N12. Heidegger's "enframing" (Gestell) of nature as standing reserve means:

✓ Correct Answer: Modern technology reveals nature only as perpetual energy/resource stock
