Modern Environmental Philosophy: Descartes to Heidegger

The epistemological turn, Cartesian dualism, fact/value split, and 20th century responses — deep ecology, environmental philosophy, and the critique of technology.

By the end

Explain Descartes' dualism, method of doubt, and why he's the "father of anti-ecological problems."

Trace

The fact/value split from Galileo/Descartes to logical positivism and its environmental consequences.

Analyze

Kant's sublime, Heidegger's enframing, and the critique of Cartesian science.

Evaluate

The emergence of environmental philosophy in 1970s America: anti-anthropocentrism.

01

Descartes: The Epistemological Turn

From medieval symbolic thinking to modern representational thinking

Medieval → Modern: Symbolic thinking → Representational thinking (images as representations of nature).
Descartes: Rationalist (reason alone like Plato). Method of doubt → "I think therefore I am." Two substances: Mind (unextended) + Matter (extended). Created by God; cannot interact → work of God.
Primary vs Secondary qualities: Primary = quantifiable/objective (extension); Secondary = subjective (color, smell). God guarantees extension in space.
Analogy: Descartes is like a security guard who locks himself in a room (doubt) until he finds one thing he can't doubt (his own thinking). Then he builds the world back up using God as the guarantor. But the world he builds is made of mathematical extension — no room for value, purpose, or life.

Figure 1: Descartes — dualism, method of doubt, primary/secondary qualities

Worked Example: Descartes' Anti-Ecological Consequences

  1. Mind/Matter split: Nature = pure extension (matter) → no intrinsic value, no purpose
  2. God sustains change: No human nature-preservation needed (God handles it)
  3. Primary qualities only: Science = math/geometry of extension → ignores life, value, time
  4. Representation problem: Our ideas vs external reality → skepticism about nature itself
  5. Hargrove: Father of modern anti-ecological philosophical problems
Exam sentence: "Descartes didn't just separate mind and body — he separated humans from nature, value from fact, and made the world a machine. Environmental philosophy is the attempt to undo this separation."
Trap: "Descartes caused the ecological crisis." Descartes provided the philosophical framework; the crisis required industrial capitalism, population growth, and technological power. Descartes made nature *thinkable* as a resource; history made it *exploitable*.
60-second teach-back: Descartes' anti-ecological legacy
  1. Mind/Matter dualism → nature = dead matter, no intrinsic value
  2. God sustains all change → no human responsibility for preservation
  3. Primary qualities (math) only → science ignores life, value, time
  4. Epistemological turn → problem of mental representation → skepticism
  5. Hargrove: "Father of modern anti-ecological philosophical problems"
02

Fact/Value Split & Science Critique

Galileo/Descartes: Primary qualities → objective knowledge; secondary qualities → subjective.
Hume: Fact = science; Value = humanities. Unbridgeable dichotomy.
Logical Positivists: Verification principle → value statements = nonsensical.
Darwin: Scientific training made him "like a machine" — injured moral/emotional character.
Hargrove: Lack of environment-friendly culture = contribution of Western philosophy.
Analogy: A surgeon who only sees anatomy (facts) and never the patient's fear (values) — technically perfect, morally blind. Modern science = that surgeon. Environmental philosophy = the patient demanding to be seen as whole.

Figure 2: Fact/value split from Galileo to logical positivism

Worked Example: Cartesian Science vs Ecology

  1. Cartesian science: Mathematical/geometrical method; measurable properties; indestructible matter; no time
  2. Ecology: Time essential; observation-based; complex systems; secondary qualities matter
  3. Conflict: Ecology treated with contempt by "real" scientists (not universal laws)
  4. Result: Ecology aligned with poets/painters — not "real science"
Exam sentence: "The fact/value split didn't just separate ethics from science — it exiled ecology from 'real' science because ecology studies secondary qualities (relationships, values, time) that Cartesian science dismisses."
60-second teach-back: Fact/value split → ecology marginalized
  1. Galileo/Descartes: primary qualities = objective; secondary = subjective
  2. Hume: fact = science, value = humanities
  3. Logical positivists: value statements = nonsensical
  4. Ecology = time, observation, secondary qualities → treated as "lesser" science
  5. Result: mainstream science too idealistic/abstract for ecology
03

Kant's Sublime & Heidegger's Enframing

Kant: Science = correlate of human experience (senses, imagination, understanding). Sublime = nature as feared power → overcome by projecting our reason → moral capacity.
Heidegger: Essence of technology ≠ technological. Technology = way nature reveals itself. Modern tech = nature as "standing reserve" (perennial energy source).
Enframing (Gestell): Nature as perpetual resource stock. Problems of technology cannot be solved technologically.
Art: Different way of encountering nature; freedom w.r.t technology.
Analogy: A forest. Science (Cartesian) = timber volume, carbon sequestration, biodiversity indices (standing reserve). Kant's sublime = awe at its vast power → moral humility. Heidegger's art = poetic dwelling in the forest → different revealing. Enframing = seeing only resources.

Figure 3: Kant's sublime, Heidegger's enframing, art as alternative

Worked Example: Heidegger on Hydroelectric Dam

  1. Enframing: River = standing reserve (energy stock) → dam reveals it as such
  2. Problem: Technology cannot be solved technologically (better turbines ≠ solve enframing)
  3. Solution: Different revealing — art, poetic dwelling, "letting be"
  4. Key: We are not agents of technology; we are driven by it
Exam sentence: "Heidegger doesn't say 'stop technology' — he says technology is a *way of revealing* that blocks other revealings. The environmental crisis is a crisis of *revealing*, not engineering."
60-second teach-back: Kant's sublime + Heidegger's enframing

Kant: Sublime = nature as feared power → we overcome fear via reason → moral capacity
Heidegger: Technology = way nature reveals itself; modern tech = standing reserve (resource stock)
Enframing: Nature as perpetual energy reserve
Art: Alternative revealing; freedom from technology
Key: Problems of tech cannot be solved technologically

04

Environmental Philosophy Emerges (1970s)

Crisis: Western philosophy = anti-ecological (solipsism, anthropocentrism, Cartesian science).
Environmental Philosophy (1970s USA): (1) Get rid of human-centrism from philosophy/culture. (2) Modernity/development must allow planet to live on.
Key figures: Arne Naess (deep ecology), Holmes Rolston, J. Baird Callicott, Bryan Norton.
Core project: Rethink metaphysics, ethics, epistemology from ecological ground up.
Analogy: A house built on rotten foundations (Cartesian metaphysics). Environmental philosophy doesn't just patch walls — it asks whether the foundation itself must be replaced.

Figure 4: Environmental philosophy emergence — anti-anthropocentrism, new metaphysics

Exam sentence: "Environmental philosophy didn't just add 'environment' to philosophy — it demanded philosophy rethink its categories: what exists, what matters, what we can know, when nature is not a machine but a community."
60-second teach-back: Environmental philosophy 1970s

Diagnosis: Western philosophy = anti-ecological (solipsism, anthropocentrism, Cartesian science)
Prescription: (1) Remove human-centrism, (2) Tailor modernity for planetary survival
Key figures: Naess (deep ecology), Rolston, Callicott, Norton
Project: Rethink metaphysics/ethics/epistemology from ecological ground up

Module 11 Practice MCQs

M11-1. Descartes' "method of doubt" leads to which foundational principle?

Descartes doubted everything until he reached the indubitable principle: "I think therefore I am" (Cogito, ergo sum). This became the foundation for rebuilding knowledge, with God guaranteeing the external world.">
✓ Correct Answer: "I think therefore I am" (Cogito, ergo sum)

M11-2. In Descartes' dualism, the two substances are:

Descartes: two non-permanent substances created by God — Mind (res cogitans, not extended in space) and Matter (res extensa, extended in space). They cannot interact directly (work of God). Primary/secondary qualities are properties of mental representation, not substances.">
✓ Correct Answer: Mind (unextended) and Matter (extended)

M11-3. The "fact/value dichotomy" in Western philosophy was reinforced by:

Chain: Galileo/Descartes emphasized primary qualities for objective knowledge → Hume: fact = science, value = humanities → Logical positivists: value statements = nonsensical (verification principle). This created the unbridgeable fact/value dichotomy that marginalized environmental ethics.">
✓ Correct Answer: Galileo/Descartes' primary/secondary qualities distinction → Hume's fact/value split → logical positivism

M11-4. Heidegger's concept of "enframing" (Gestell) means:

Heidegger: essence of technology is not technological. Technology is a way nature reveals itself. Modern technology = enframing = nature as standing reserve (perennial energy source). Problems of technology cannot be solved technologically; need different revealing (art).">
✓ Correct Answer: Modern technology reveals nature as a standing reserve — a perpetual energy/resource stock

M11-5. The emergence of environmental philosophy in 1970s America aimed to:

Environmental philosophy (1970s USA) had two core aims per Hargrove: (1) Get rid of human-centrism from philosophy and culture, (2) Modernity and development must be tailored to allow planet and environment to live on. It was a philosophical reorientation, not policy or economics.">
✓ Correct Answer: Get rid of human-centrism from philosophy and tailor modernity for planetary survival
