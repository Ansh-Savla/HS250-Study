01

Indian Approaches: Realist, Idealist & Nature's Sentience

Realist: Perceived objects exist independent of consciousness (Nyaya-Vaisheshika, Mimamsa).
Idealist: Object aware of itself? Consciousness constitutes reality (Advaita, Yogacara).
Nature's sentience: Is nature sentient or insentient? Does it hurt? Retaliate? Shared responsibility of not hurting nature? → Question of ethics.
Sustainable practices: Study socio-religious practices for ecological balance.
Analogy: A river. Realist: river exists whether anyone sees it. Idealist: river's existence depends on consciousness. Sentience question: does the river feel pain when polluted? Should I treat it as a conscious being? Ethics emerges from how we answer.

Figure 1: Realist vs Idealist, sentience question, Buddhist core doctrines

Worked Example: Ganga as Legal Person (2017)

  1. Legal innovation: Uttarakhand HC declared Ganga + Yamuna living entities with legal rights
  2. Philosophical root: Indian Idealist view — nature as conscious/sentient
  3. Realist critique: Rivers don't have consciousness; legal fiction ≠ ontological fact
  4. Ethical implication: If nature sentient → harming it = direct moral wrong, not just instrumental harm
Exam sentence: "The Indian sentience debate isn't abstract — it shaped the 2017 Ganga legal personhood ruling. Whether nature is a 'who' or a 'what' changes everything about environmental law."
60-second teach-back: Realist vs Idealist + Sentience

Realist: Objects exist independent of mind (Nyaya, Mimamsa)
Idealist: Consciousness constitutes reality (Advaita, Yogacara)
Sentience: Is nature conscious? Does it hurt? → Ethics of shared responsibility
Ganga 2017: Legal personhood reflects Idealist/sentience view

02

Buddhist Philosophy: Dependent Origination & Panchsheel

Experiential knowing: Verify truth yourself; impermanence (anicca) of existence.
Dependent origination (Pratītyasamutpāda): Universe = mutual web of relationships; no independent existence.
Panchsheel (5 Precepts): Not to kill, not to lie, not to steal, not to abuse sexually, no intoxication/drugs.
Bodhisattva: Vow to save all beings. Three Jewels: Buddha (teaches), Dharma (truth), Sangha (community).
Analogy: Indra's Net — infinite net of jewels, each reflecting all others. Touch one jewel → all vibrate. This is dependent origination: nothing exists independently; harm to one = harm to whole web.

Figure 2: Buddhist core — experiential knowing, dependent origination, Panchsheel, Three Jewels

Worked Example: Buddhist Environmental Ethics in Action

  1. Thai forest monks: Ordained trees (wrapped in saffron robes) → logging = harming a monk
  2. Mechanism: Experiential knowing → trees as sentient → Panchsheel (not to kill) applies
  3. Dependent origination: Forest = web of life; logging tears the web → collective harm
  4. Result: Thousands of hectares protected; community-based conservation
Exam sentence: "Buddhist environmental ethics isn't 'nature worship' — it's applied dependent origination: if all things are mutually dependent, harming nature IS harming oneself."
60-second teach-back: Buddhist core doctrines

Experiential knowing: Verify truth yourself; impermanence
Dependent origination: Universe = mutual web; no independent existence
Panchsheel: Not kill, lie, steal, sexual abuse, intoxication
Bodhisattva: Vow to save all beings
3 Jewels: Buddha (teacher), Dharma (truth), Sangha (community)

03

Buddhist View ≠ Oneness + Kant/Heidegger in Indian Context

Buddhist view ≠ Oneness: Not monism (Advaita). View of differences + multiplicities WITH interconnectedness. No "One" ontology.
Kant in India: Modern science = correlate of human experience (senses, imagination, understanding). Sublime = nature as feared power → overcome by reason → morality. Science disconnects us from nature.
Heidegger in India: Technology = way nature reveals itself. Enframing = nature as standing reserve. Art = alternative revealing (freedom from tech).
Analogy: Advaita = ocean (all waves = one water). Buddhism = Indra's Net (distinct jewels, each reflecting all). Both see interconnectedness, but Buddhism preserves difference — crucial for environmental ethics (each species matters distinctly).

Figure 3: Buddhist view ≠ Oneness; Kant's sublime & Heidegger's enframing in Indian context

Worked Example: Buddhist Climate Ethics

  1. Impermanence: Climate change = large-scale impermanence → suffering (dukkha)
  2. Dependent origination: Emissions anywhere → impacts everywhere (web of relations)
  3. Non-harming: Panchsheel (not to kill) → fossil fuel extraction = killing future beings
  4. Compassion: Bodhisattva vow → act for all beings, not just humans
  5. Middle Way: Neither denial nor despair → engaged action
Exam sentence: "Buddhist environmental ethics = dependent origination applied to climate: if all beings are mutually dependent, carbon emissions anywhere harm beings everywhere — including the emitter."
60-second teach-back: Buddhist ≠ Oneness + Kant/Heidegger

Buddhist ≠ Oneness: Differences + multiplicities WITH interconnectedness (Indra's Net, not ocean)
Kant: Sublime = nature as feared power → overcome by reason → moral capacity
Heidegger: Enframing = nature as standing reserve; art = alternative revealing
Science: Disconnects us from nature; we become controllers, not participants

04

Ecofeminism: Dualisms, Domination & Liberation

Ecofeminism = Ecological + Feminist + Philosophical positions.
Main premise: Domination of others (human/non-human) by dominant group is NOT inevitable, NOT justified.
Core link: Unjustified domination of women ↔ domination of nature → THESE INTERSECT.
Feminization of nature / Naturalization of women: Women = Nature, Body, Emotion; Men = Culture, Mind, Reason.
Analogy: A tree and a woman. Patriarchal culture says: both are passive resources to be managed by men (culture/mind/reason). Ecofeminism says: this parallel devaluation is the ROOT of both ecological destruction and gender oppression. Liberate one → must liberate both.

Figure 4: Ecofeminism — dualisms, oppressive framework, logic of domination

Worked Example: Chipko as Ecofeminist Action

  1. Women-led: Village women hugged trees (Gaura Devi, Bachni Devi)
  2. Connection: Women = primary resource gatherers (water, fuel, fodder) → deforestation hurts women first
  3. Dualism challenged: Nature = passive resource (male view) vs Nature = life-giver (women's experience)
  4. Outcome: 15-year logging ban; inspired global ecofeminist movements
Exam sentence: "Ecofeminism doesn't just add 'women' to environmentalism — it shows that the SAME conceptual framework (hierarchy, dualisms, domination) drives both ecological destruction and gender oppression."
60-second teach-back: Ecofeminism core

Equation: Ecofeminism = Ecological + Feminist + Philosophical
Premise: Domination of nature & women by same framework → NOT inevitable, NOT justified
Dualisms: Women/Nature, Body/Mind, Emotion/Reason = devalued pairs
Oppressive framework: Value-hierarchy (X>Y), oppositional dualisms, top-down power, logic of domination
Ecofeminist response: Reject domination logic; reject differences as justification for domination

Module 12 Practice MCQs

M12-1. In Indian philosophy, the Realist position holds that:

Realist (Nyaya, Mimamsa): perceived objects really do exist, irrespective of the viewer's consciousness. Idealist (Advaita, Yogacara): consciousness constitutes reality; object aware of itself? This is the core debate.">
✓ Correct Answer: Perceived objects exist independent of the viewer's consciousness

M12-2. Buddhist philosophy's "dependent origination" (pratītyasamutpāda) means:

Dependent origination = universe is a mutual web of relationships; no independent existence (like Indra's Net). NOT oneness/monism (that's Advaita). Buddhism = differences + multiplicities WITH interconnectedness. 'No notion of the one.'">
✓ Correct Answer: Universe is a mutual web of relationships; nothing exists independently

M12-3. The Panchsheel (five precepts) in Buddhism are:

Panchsheel = 5 ethical precepts for lay Buddhists: (1) Not to kill, (2) Not to lie, (3) Not to steal, (4) Not to abuse sexually, (5) Not to indulge in intoxication/drugs. The Eightfold Path is the path to liberation; Noble Truths are the diagnosis. Panchsheel is the ethical foundation.">
✓ Correct Answer: Not to kill, not to lie, not to steal, not to abuse sexually, not to indulge in intoxication/drugs

M12-4. Ecofeminism's "oppressive conceptual framework" includes all EXCEPT:

Oppressive framework = value-hierarchical thinking (Man > Woman, Culture > Nature), oppositional dualisms (Nature/Culture, Body/Mind, Emotion/Reason), top-down power model, logic of domination (X has feature → superior → domination justified). 'Egalitarian partnership' is what ecofeminism ADVOCATES, not part of the oppressive framework.">
✓ Correct Answer: Egalitarian partnership model

M12-5. The Buddhist view of "not oneness" means:

Buddhist perspective ≠ view of oneness (that's Advaita Vedanta). Buddhism = view of differences and multiplicities WITH their interconnectedness. Pratītyasamutpāda = mutual web of relationships; no single 'One' ontology. Distinct entities, mutually dependent. Indra's Net metaphor: distinct jewels, each reflecting all others.">
✓ Correct Answer: View of differences and multiplicities WITH their interconnectedness (Indra's Net)
